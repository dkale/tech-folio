# Abstraction vs Encapsulation

**Abstraction vs. Encapsulation**

Abstraction and encapsulation are two fundamental object-oriented programming (OOP) concepts, and they serve different purposes in software design.

**Abstraction**:

- Abstraction is the process of simplifying complex real-world entities by focusing on their essential characteristics and hiding unnecessary details.

- In OOP, **abstraction is achieved through abstract classes and interfaces**, which define a common set of properties and methods that concrete classes must implement.

- Abstraction allows you to create a blueprint or template for objects without specifying their complete implementation.

- It enables you to define the structure and behaviour of objects at a higher level, making the design more manageable and allowing you to work with objects at a conceptual level rather than getting bogged down in implementation details.

- Abstraction helps in achieving **separation of concerns** and promotes **modularity and extensibility** in the code.

**Encapsulation**:

- Encapsulation is the **concept of bundling data (attributes or fields) and methods (behaviours or functions)** that operate on the data within a single unit (i.e., a class).

- The main idea behind encapsulation is to **hide the internal implementation details of an object** and expose a well-defined interface for interacting with the object.

- Access to the internal data of an object is controlled through access modifiers (e.g., public, private, protected) to prevent unauthorised or unintended access and modification of the object's state.

- Encapsulation helps in achieving data protection and data hiding, making the objects more robust and secure. It also prevents direct manipulation of an object's internal state by external code, promoting a more controlled and predictable behaviour of the object.

In summary, abstraction is about representing the essential characteristics of an object and hiding unnecessary details, while encapsulation is about bundling data and methods together to control access and protect the internal state of an object. Both concepts are crucial in OOP and contribute to building well-designed, maintainable, and scalable software. They help in managing complexity and promoting code reusability and flexibility.
